# Concepts

1. Pop and Shift 

 

Input: Webcam, hand/motion tracking 

 

What changes: A bubble floats over your live reflection; popping it (hand overlaps its position) bursts into a new visual filter over your reflection, and a new bubble appears. 

 

How the viewer knows: The bubble hopefully reads as "things to pop," so the first pop teaches the whole interaction. Once it happens once, they’ll probably want to do it again. 

 

2. Draw With the Computer 

Input: Webcam or mouse, hand/motion tracking 

 

What changes: You draw a simple shape in the air (or on screen); the piece tries to guess what you drew and displays its guess as text or a redrawn image, like a live game of Pictionary against the machine. 

 

How the viewer knows: A blank "canvas" area and a visible cursor/trail following the hand reads as "draw here”... it could also literally say “draw” 

 

 

3. Speech to Song 

Input: Microphone (rhythm/amplitude from any sound — talking, singing, humming, clapping) 

 

What changes: The piece detects the rhythm and emphasis of the sound made and plays it back as a percussive/melodic pattern on a selected instrument once the sound stops. swiping through icons at the top of the screen could change which instrument is used. 

 

How the viewer knows: Making a sound produces an immediate, audible playback matching its rhythm, and the instrument icons at the top provide a visual connection about what’s happening. 

 

 

 

 

4. Stretch Break Mirror 

 

Input: Webcam, pose tracking 

 

What changes: An on-screen guide figure shows a stretch pose; matching the pose (rough alignment of your silhouette to the outline) starts a countdown. Once countdown is over, a new silhouette is shown to stretch. 

 

How the viewer knows: The outline is a "match this shape" cue. 

 

5. Everything You Say, Drawn 

Input: Microphone (voice/amplitude and pitch) 

 

What changes: Speaking generates abstract shapes/lines on screen in real time based on the pitch/volume/rhythm of the voice, building up a drawing over the course of a conversation. 

 

How the viewer knows: There is a  direct sync between talking and marks appearing, so hopefully people naturally keep talking to see what else they can "draw." 

 

At the end of this you get a sort of painting of you speaking (it would be really cool with a poem or something maybe? It would also be interesting with a conversation- two voices could be different colors or shapes or something) 

 

6. Puppeteer 

 

Input: Webcam, hand/pose tracking  

 

What changes: The viewer initially sees their full body on screen. After stepping closer to the camera and positioning their hands into a specific puppeteer pose, their hands are detected and rendered at the top of the screen with glowing outlines that show where to place them. Once they match the position, their live body is replaced by a puppet character with their face as the puppet's head and a stylized puppet body underneath. The viewer can then control the puppet by moving their hands in the designated positions, making the puppet's arms and body move accordingly.  

 

How the viewer knows: The initial full-body view establishes the camera interaction. When the viewer steps forward, hands appear as glowing outlines (tbd) at the top of the screen, clearly prompting them to move their hands into position. Once they match the outlines, the transformation into a puppet with their own face makes the interaction immediately understandable: their hands are controlling a digital version of themselves. 

 

 

7. Catch the bugs 

Input: Webcam, motion/hand tracking 

 

What changes: Bugs drift around the screen; swiping your hand through its position "catches" it in a digital net.  

 

How the viewer knows: the viewer knows to try to catch the bugs, because as soon as they put their hand up to the screen a net overlays their hand 

 

 

8. Feed the Critter 

Input: Hand tracking  

 

What changes: A little creature lives in the middle of the screen. You use your hand to “feed” it virtual objects that appear around you. It reacts based on what you give it.  

 

How the viewer knows: The creature looks toward nearby objects and opens its mouth when your hand gets close.  

 

 

9. The Floor Is Lava 

Input: Webcam/pose tracking 

 

What changes: The screen detects your feet and creates a virtual environment underneath you. Certain areas become “dangerous,” and you have to move your feet into safe zones.  

 

How the viewer knows: It will feel like a video game pattern they are hopefully familiar with where they have to avoid certain things. (like crossy road or something similar) 

 

 

10. Plant Yourself 

Input: Hand tracking  

 

What changes: The viewer uses their hand to plant seeds throughout the screen. The seeds quickly grow into flowers, with the viewer's face appearing in the center of each flower. The flowers react to the viewer's current facial expressions, so smiling, frowning, or moving their face makes all of the flowers react too.  

 

How the viewer knows: A seed appears wherever the viewer places their hand, then immediately grows into a flower. Seeing their own face appear inside the flower reinforces that they are creating the flowers themselves.  

 

11. Color Thief 

Input: Hand tracking  

 

What changes: Illustrated objects continuously fall from the top of the screen. When the viewer touches an object with their hand, they “steal” its color or pattern, causing their on-screen body to transform to match it.  

How the viewer knows: The objects visibly fall toward them, and touching one immediately transfers its color or pattern onto their body.  

 

12. Copycat Creature 

Input: Webcam / body or pose tracking  

 

What changes: A cute creature on screen watches the viewer and copies their movements with a slight delay. As the viewer moves, the creature gradually mimics their poses or gestures.  

 

How the viewer knows: The creature visibly follows their movements right away. The slight delay makes it clear that the user is being copied.

# Using these ideas in a branding context?

## Feed the Critter

This one could translate well into a certain brand if the critter itself was the mascot, and the items being "fed" to it by the user are the product being sold.

## Color Thief 

"Color Thief" could turn into "Product Thief". For example, if it was being used by Adidas, different Adidas products could be falling from the top of the screen, and if the user taps on them, it lets them simulate trying them on. This could also work for any brand that has different colors for products (beauty,fashion) and could let someone figure out their color season by "trying on" a fill of color that corresponds with the company's products.

## Plant yourself

If this were at an event with multiple people, this interaction could create a "garden" of everyone who interacted. The user selects a seed once, and they "plant themselves" in the shared garden.
